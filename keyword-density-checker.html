What is keyword density?

Keyword density is the share of a text that one word or phrase makes up. If a phrase appears 5 times in a text of 500 words, its density is 1 percent. The number is only a description of the text. It does not tell you how a page will rank.

Search engines do not publish an ideal percentage, and one number never proves keyword stuffing. The useful part is spotting wording that repeats more than you meant to.

How to use the keyword density checker

  1. Paste your text into the box, or press Load demo to try an example.
  2. Add a focus keyword (optional) to see how often it appears and whether it is in the first 100 words.
  3. Read the table. Switch between 1, 2 and 3 word phrases and use the search box to find a phrase.
  4. Check the stats for words, sentences, reading time and a readability estimate.
  5. Export a CSV if you want to work with the numbers in a spreadsheet.

How to read the results

  • Count and density: how many times a word or phrase appears, and its share of all words.
  • Colors: green up to 2.5 percent, amber above 2.5 percent and red above 3.5 percent. These are rough review cues made by this tool, not Google rules.
  • Stop words: common English words such as the and of can be left out so the topic words stand out.
  • Readability: the Flesch score is an estimate for English text. Other languages show no score.

Tips for natural writing

  • Write for the reader first, then use the checker to review the result.
  • Use the main phrase where it helps, such as in the title, a heading and the introduction.
  • If a phrase repeats a lot, read those sentences in context and vary the wording.
  • Do not chase a number. Answer the question clearly and add useful details.

Frequently asked questions

What is keyword density?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears in a text compared with the total number of words, shown as a percentage. This tool calculates it as occurrences divided by total words, multiplied by 100.

Is there an ideal keyword density?

No. Google does not publish an ideal percentage, and a number alone does not prove keyword stuffing. Use the figures to spot repeated wording, then check that the text reads naturally and answers the reader's question.

What do the colors in the table mean?

They are rough review cues set by this tool: green up to 2.5 percent, amber above 2.5 percent and red above 3.5 percent. They are not Google rules or penalty thresholds.

Which languages does it support?

It finds words in any language that uses letters and numbers, including Urdu, Hindi, other non-Latin scripts and accented European languages, and it counts phrases of one to three words. The stop word list and the readability score work for English only.

How is the Flesch Reading Ease score calculated?

It uses the average sentence length and the average number of syllables per word. The syllable count is an estimate that works best for English, so treat the score as a rough guide. Higher scores are easier to read, and 60 to 70 is often described as plain English.

Why exclude stop words?

Words such as the, of and to appear in almost every text and would fill the top of the table. Excluding them shows the topic words instead. The stop word list is English only.

Is my text uploaded or saved?

No. The analysis runs in your browser, your text is not sent to Ruvixsa and the page does not save it.

Are there any limits?

To keep your browser fast, only the first 300,000 characters are analyzed. The table shows the 50 most frequent entries, and the CSV download contains all of them.
